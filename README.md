# flittle

The flittle website: one page, plain HTML, CSS and ES modules, no build step.

```bash
npx serve .        # then open the printed URL
```

Any static server works. The cover mixes illustrated landscapes and cinematic backgrounds using one WebGL surface. The supplied MP4s stream from their original CloudFront URLs; the village and procedural galaxy are local. Browsers without WebGL get static artwork and the same click-to-change theme interaction.

## What is on the page

1. **Entrance, arcade and cover** (`index.html`, `intro.css`, `lib/intro.js`, `lib/intro-invitation.js`, `lib/intro-title.js`, `lib/intro-field.js`, `lib/arcade-runtime.js`, `lib/arcade-art.js`, `lib/arcade-dodge.js`, `lib/arcade-flight.js`, `lib/arcade-action.js`): Neon blocks animate on black while the whole website prepares: cover, decoded images, fonts, sculpture, project textures and GPU effects. The initial progress indicator follows completed tasks; failed essential loads offer Retry Loading. The pixel “Begin your journey” invitation accepts a click or tap anywhere, or Enter/Space. Its actual title pixels glow and gently spring away from the pointer without disappearing. A simple click hint follows the pointer with a fading pixel trail; touch screens show a fixed tap hint. A quiet pixel field responds across the entire invitation, including empty space, then settles when the pointer rests. The invitation folds into a pixel gate; small beveled tiles gather and accelerate outward to open a five-game arcade built with local Canvas 2D. `arcade-motion.css` supplies the finite launch and game-switch transitions. The invitation and games only become available after the cover and heavy website assets are ready.

   | Game | Controls |
   | --- | --- |
   | Star Dodge | Left/right or A/D moves the mascot; drag to move on touch; Space/Shift starts then shield-dashes. Dodge falling blocks and collect stars through five waves. |
   | Flittle Flight | Space, Up, click or tap flaps the mascot through the gaps. |
   | Brick Break | Left/right or A/D moves the paddle; mouse or drag aims it; Space or tap launches the mascot ball. |
   | Cosmic Flittle | Arrows/WASD fly; hold Space to fire; drag and hold to move and fire on touch screens. |
   | Little Runner | Space, Up, click or tap jumps over obstacles. |

   Side arrows, the five selector dots, or Q/E switch games. The opening invitation has no website bypass. A large Enter Website button appears once the arcade opens and stays available during every game and on results; Escape also exits after beginning. Each result keeps this button alongside the score, Play Again and game navigation. Star Dodge has three lives, telegraphed block rows with safe corridors, collectible stars and a rechargeable shield dash.

   Every game exits through `lib/arcade-exit.js` and `lib/arcade-ult.js` with a final power move using the actual game frame and mascot position. Cosmic Flittle charges a galaxy-clearing beam, Star Dodge lands a shield slam, Flight uses a winged comet dash, Brick Break chains a fiery ricochet through the remaining bricks, and Runner tears through the course with a ground-level hyperdash. Fragments and motion trails stay tied to the round's actual objects. Each attack clears the entire viewport, including the corners and floor; dashes and their wakes travel beyond the edges. The attack's energy assembles the blue-and-gold pixel mascot (`lib/arcade-portal.js`). `lib/arcade-handoff.js` then grows and moves it into the measured homepage mascot position, resolving its pixels into the existing, decoded artwork as the prepared cover appears. The real image takes over in the same frame, without a duplicate mascot or second loading screen. Returning from an arcade session further down the page keeps the saved position and uses a short fade instead. The entire departure takes roughly 2.5 to 3 seconds. Real preparation milestones in `lib/site-readiness.js` gate the initial blocks screen. Reduced motion skips the attack, mascot assembly and handoff. The departure pauses in hidden tabs and releases its canvas, listeners and animation frame on completion.

   First entry opens the colour cover at the top and clears restored section anchors. The fixed Arcade button reopens the last selected game later; leaving that session restores the previous page position and keyboard focus. One game runs at a time: switching, replaying and exiting destroy the old engine, cancel animation frames, remove canvas listeners and release held input. Hidden tabs pause updates; the website's costly scenes pause behind the arcade. Every game fills the viewport, with uniform pixel scaling and safe gameplay bounds around the overlaid controls. Resizing preserves the current round, score and lives.

   The entrance is a labelled modal with keyboard focus contained inside it, labelled game and touch controls, live game/score announcements, an accessible loading progress bar and visible focus indicators. The background is inert while it is open; the hidden arcade cannot intercept input after exit. A failed game still offers direct entry to the prepared website. Readiness timeouts report failure and never declare unfinished graphics ready; retry reloads cleanly without creating duplicate renderers.

   The cover uses `lib/preload.js`, `hero.css`, `lib/boot.js`, `lib/ink.js`, `lib/hero-neon.js` and the scene registry/media player in `lib/hero-worlds.js`. Six worlds share one shuffled pool: Village, Wonder, Galaxy, Event Horizon, Rose Nebula and Violet Orbit. There are no day/night categories, Crimson clips or skyscraper scene. Each world gives both the lettering and mascot a distinct material, while preserving the original silhouette and readable face:

   | World | Wordmark and mascot material |
   | --- | --- |
   | Village | Meadow blades bend locally in a breeze as warm sunlight moves across them; the mascot catches a lighter moss tone, with earth-colored feet. |
   | Wonder | Soft, drifting cloud banks sampled from its lavender sky, with pearl-lit puffs and deeper lilac shadows; the mascot has a richer lavender tint, rose nose and champagne eyes. |
   | Galaxy | Luminous cyan metal with traveling signals along its circuit tracks and a moving sheen; a deeper blue circuit-covered mascot with pink accents. |
   | Event Horizon | Moving accretion wisps sampled from the actual black-hole film, unwrapped across obsidian and wine-red lettering with crimson filaments and hot ember highlights; warm ivory eyes and a vermilion nose. The material preserves the film's orientation and follows its playback directly, matching the background's rotation. A red grade ties the background to the material while preserving black space. |
   | Rose Nebula | Moving rose plasma sampled from the actual planet film, unwrapped across the lettering and mascot; dark wine cloud bands, pink atmospheric rims and pale rose facial details. |
   | Violet Orbit | Deep violet glass with slowly moving curved reflections, concentrated glossy highlights and a lavender inner rim. Sparse, faint mesh seams sit beneath the glass; the mascot is slightly lighter royal violet with pearl-blue accents. |

   `lib/hero-digital-materials.js`, `lib/hero-natural-materials.js` and `lib/hero-city-material.js` supply these surfaces. Their movement stays inside the original logo silhouette and preserves the mascot's face. The grass canvas (`lib/hero-grass.js`) and cloud tile (`lib/hero-cloud-texture.js`) are cached once. Wonder's cloud map uses its already decoded sky artwork, with no extra download or video player. Both maps reuse each world's unused overlay sampler, keeping the renderer at eight texture units. Rose Nebula and Event Horizon reuse their existing film textures and loop crossfades, so their moving materials need no additional download or video decoder. Their polar crops follow the moving atmosphere and pause with the background, including reduced motion. Only Rose Nebula adds a gentle independent drift; Event Horizon follows the background video directly. Static non-WebGL fallbacks retain the original multicolor mascot and themed lettering.

   Village uses `assets/village-day-v1.png`, with gentle foliage, cloud and water movement. Two prepared players crossfade each film's forward-playing loop; only the current loop runs outside that overlap. Rose Nebula and Violet Orbit crossfade over 1.0 and 0.9 seconds. Wonder uses the open crystal portal, lavender landscape, curtains and clouds from Step Into Wonder, with a foreground composed once and gentle pointer parallax. The duplicate Web3 and Wonder prompts each share one entry. Reverie's Cloudinary artwork returned HTTP 401, so it has no separate scene yet. Original Wonder assets and their source URLs are in `assets/worlds/wonder/`; local copies allow same-origin WebGL sampling.

   A new visit randomly chooses a scene, avoiding the last selected scene when session storage is available. Any other scene can be its hover reveal. Clicking or tapping the cover expands the preview circle from that point to fill the screen in about 500 ms, visiting the remaining scenes in shuffled order before repeating. Only two scenes stay prepared. The current scene remains visible while the next one loads; replaced players are disposed, overlapping changes are prevented, and a pending change is cancelled when the arcade reopens. A failed change preserves the scene and lets the user retry. The initial blocks loader waits for both selected scenes to decode and upload; unavailable remote media falls back to the local galaxy. Films stay muted and inline, pause offscreen/behind the arcade/in hidden tabs, and remain still with reduced motion. Hovering or dragging reveals the other scene with its own logo finish. The arcade captures the selected material into its mascot handoff without a blue-mascot flash, and returning from the arcade preserves the theme. `assets/mascot.svg` also supplies the mascot in project details. The village artwork's generation prompt is in `assets/village-day-v1.prompt.txt`.

2. **Story** (`style.css`, `tokens.css`, `lib/cloud.js`, `cursor.js`, `liquid.js`, `scroll.js`, `frame.js`, `reveal.js`, `veil.js`, `tone.js`): the point-cloud bust that follows the pointer, the pinned zoom-out with the flying widgets, then the Services and Approach screens. The bust and dashboard share a short scroll glide that bridges discrete wheel notches without changing native page scrolling. Panel entries ease in and out; pointer motion retains its springs. Moving widgets use solid surfaces and gain a fixed glass treatment once settled; their transforms and opacity update independently. Layout measurements are cached and render resolution is bounded; render loops pause behind the entrance and after leaving the scene. Reduced motion, navigation jumps and layout changes snap the visual scroll position to its destination.
3. **Work** (`lib/orbit.js`, `lib/hold.js`, `lib/focus.js`, `lib/projects.js`): on desktop, the bust bursts into dust and its surviving points become the stars of the orbit, a sphere with the project cards around it (the projects repeat around the ring until it is full). For a mouse or trackpad, the page freezes on this section: the wheel spins the orbit and drag turns it. Hover reads a project, and a click brings it into focus: the card flies out to hang in front of the camera under fog and grain while its story decodes in, terminal style, with the links as "[name] ↗". Close, Escape or the backdrop send it back. The two arrows in the pill at the bottom leave the section. Coarse-pointer devices and initial viewports under 768px instead use `lib/portfolio-mobile.js` and `portfolio-mobile.css`: a native swipe/arrow viewer with the same four project images, stories, tags and links. It has ordinary vertical scrolling, no orbit renderer or scroll hold, and visible navigation to Connect with us. The initial mode persists through rotation; desktop orbit code and styling are unchanged. Project images decode during initial loading in either mode.
4. **Contact** (`contact.css`, `lib/contact.js`, `lib/contact-art.js`): a simplified Doodle Crew sign-off with the FLITTLE wordmark, interactive hand-drawn characters, and the link to Flittle's LinkedIn page (`https://www.linkedin.com/company/flittle/`), the way to get in touch. Each letter has its own hover, tap and keyboard animation, independent of its entrance. The crew follows the pointer and responds to clicks or Enter/Space; reduced motion is respected.

The cover itself changes themes on click or tap, with a quiet cue in the bottom-left corner. The compact **Arcade** icon remains at the top. The cover and Arcade control support keyboard and touch; Enter or Space changes the theme when the cover has focus. Scene names are announced to screen readers; there is no visible scene label or counter. For direct previews, use `?scene=village`, `?scene=galaxy`, `?scene=web3`, `?scene=wonder`, `?scene=moxly` or `?scene=sellix`. Future footage is a registry entry in `lib/hero-worlds.js` with its rendering kind, URL, crop and material; loading, looping, hover and pause behavior stay shared.

The cover renderer calculates only visible logo materials, reuses texture storage and uploads the trail only while it changes. Video textures and their materials follow actual frame callbacks (with a playback-time fallback in older browsers), and the callbacks are cancelled when a player is disposed. Cloud drift updates at 30 fps while visible; the village, galaxy and interactive reveals are capped at 60 updates per second. All material animation pauses behind the arcade, offscreen, in hidden tabs and with reduced motion. Render resolution has a two-million-pixel budget that steps down on sustained slow frames, with a floor of 850,000 pixels. Original media and theme artwork are unchanged. Fallback posters are encoded only for browsers without WebGL.

## Deploying

The repo deploys on Vercel as plain static files. `vercel.json` sets the framework to "Other" (`framework: null`), which overrides the project's old Next.js preset, so there is no install or build step and the repo root is served as is. Vercel only deploys commits whose author is a member of the Vercel account that owns the project; a commit from anyone else shows up on GitHub as "Deployment was blocked" and the owner has to redeploy it or add that person to the team.

## Notes

- Sizes are design pixels (`--u` in `tokens.css`), so the layout keeps its proportions at any width; the phone layout kicks in under 768 px.
- The bust model is three.js's `Zaghetto.pcd`, served locally from `assets/scene/zaghetto.pcd` along with the dashboard's widget images; the fonts come from Google Fonts and jsDelivr; three.js comes from jsDelivr through the import map in `index.html`.
- `favicon.svg`, `favicon-32.png` and `apple-touch-icon.png` are the mascot cat from the wordmark.
- `window.__flittle.state()`, `window.__kin` and the read-only `window.__flittleIntro.state()` are exposed for automated checks only.
